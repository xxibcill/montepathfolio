## Design Context

### Users
Learners using Montepathfolio strictly for education: financially curious people, students, and project reviewers who want to understand portfolio uncertainty and Monte Carlo simulation without needing to master every statistical detail first. Their job is to construct hypothetical stock-and-bond scenarios, inspect the range of simulated outcomes, and learn how assumptions affect upside and downside. The product must never encourage using its outputs for real investments, forecasts, or financial advice.

### Brand Personality
Calm, rigorous, candid. The interface should make uncertainty approachable, encourage thoughtful exploration, and explain risk in plain language without creating false confidence or presenting simulation output as a prediction, recommendation, or real-world investment plan.

### Aesthetic Direction
A refined editorial research notebook: warm paper neutrals, ink-like typography, restrained forest green and vermilion signals, precise chart annotation, and visible methodological context. Support both light and dark modes with equal care; dark mode should feel like the same quiet research instrument in lower light, not a separate visual identity. Keep the interface highly legible and data-dense without resembling a trading terminal. Avoid cyberpunk aesthetics, neon fintech gradients, glassmorphism, generic card grids, casino-like market imagery, and overly corporate dashboard chrome.

### Design Principles
1. Teach before suggesting: frame every scenario as an educational experiment, keep limitations visible, and never imply a forecast, recommendation, or basis for real investment.
2. Show uncertainty honestly: ranges, probabilities, and assumptions are primary; single values are secondary and never presented as promises.
3. Explain cause and effect: make each scenario change and its simulated impact legible in plain language while preserving the prior run for fair comparison.
4. Reveal complexity progressively: keep the core simulation approachable and place modeling details, terminology, and limitations close at hand.
5. Meet WCAG 2.2 AA across light and dark modes: preserve keyboard and screen-reader access, reduced-motion support, color-independent chart encodings, readable contrast, and responsive behavior from mobile through wide desktop.
