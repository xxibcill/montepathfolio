## Design Context

### Users
Financially curious, self-directed investors and portfolio-project reviewers who want to compare long-term allocation choices without needing to understand every statistical detail first. Their primary job is to build a simple stock-and-bond scenario, see the range of plausible outcomes, and understand how a change affects both upside and downside.

### Brand Personality
Calm, rigorous, candid. The interface should create informed confidence without implying certainty, and explain risk in plain language rather than presenting simulation output as a prediction.

### Aesthetic Direction
A refined editorial research notebook: warm paper neutrals, ink-like typography, restrained green and vermilion signals, precise chart annotation, and visible methodological context. Light-first, highly legible, and data-dense without resembling a trading terminal. Avoid neon fintech gradients, glassmorphism, generic card grids, casino-like market imagery, and overly corporate dashboard chrome.

### Design Principles
1. Put the decision before the data: lead with what changed and why it matters.
2. Show uncertainty honestly: ranges and probabilities are primary; single values are secondary.
3. Use progressive disclosure: keep the core scenario approachable and place modeling details close at hand.
4. Make comparisons legible: preserve the prior run so every adjustment can produce a plain-language delta.
5. Keep controls and charts responsive, keyboard-accessible, color-safe, and useful from mobile through wide desktop.
